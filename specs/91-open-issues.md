# Open issues

Discrepancies between the specification and the code, unfinished work, unresolved questions.
Each entry states what diverged, where it was found, and what decision is needed.
Entries are removed once the maintainer decides which side is right and the fix lands.

Entries are numbered `Q-NNN` in tens so that a requirement's rationale can name one; a number is never reused.
Sixteen questions were opened on 2026-09-21 by the decomposition of the concept and fourteen were settled the same day; each settlement is an ADR, so a closed number is found by searching `specs/adr/` for it: Q-010 and Q-090 in ADR-0009, Q-020 and Q-030 and Q-050 and Q-060 in ADR-0010, Q-070 and Q-080 in ADR-0013, Q-100 in ADR-0011, Q-110 in ADR-0014, Q-130 in ADR-0012, Q-150 in ADR-0015; Q-040 became FR-SNAKE-220, Q-140 a sentence in NFR-PERF-010's rationale and Q-160 FR-VIEW-120.

Two more were opened and settled on 2026-09-22 by the maintainer: Q-190 — whether a severed tail segment stays in the arena — became H-080, and Q-200 — the hypothesis the shedding requirements rest on — became H-070, both in `grounds/10-h-mechanics.md`; a question that turns into a measurement is closed here and answered there.

### Q-120 — The name is chosen; the search that clears it is not done

- **Found:** concept §14, §17.6; CON-PUB-090.
- **What diverged:** the maintainer settled the name as "Crawl Call: Chomp Company" on 2026-09-22, deliberately ahead of the trademark search rather than after it. The reason the search was asked for stands: "Chomp" is taken in apps (a children's app, a Google Play game, a UK studio), and "Crawl Call" — the half that carries the mark — has not been searched at all.
- **Decided:** on 2026-09-21 the order was search first, name after. On 2026-09-22 the maintainer reversed it and named the game, which changes what the search decides — no longer which name to take, but whether this one survives. A professional search in classes 9 and 41, EU first, is still commissioned before any public announcement, the name appears nowhere outside the repository until then (CON-PUB-090), and registration, if it clears, is by the company (ADR-0007).
- **Still needed:** the search itself — an action outside the repository, in classes 9 and 41, EU first, on "Crawl Call" before the full string. The entry closes when the result is recorded here.
  If it does not clear, what changes is not only the name: the code short name `CrawlCall` — the Xcode target, the source folder and the tail of the bundle identifier — goes with it, which is the cost of having named the game first.

### Q-250 — The frame rate has been looked at, not measured

- **Found:** NFR-PERF-010; the Metal performance HUD on 2026-09-24.
- **What diverged:** NFR-PERF-010 is verified by `A`, which `specs/50-verification.md` defines as an unattended run along a fixed route with the telemetry pulled from the device afterwards. The rest of the game's own instrument exists since 2026-09-24: the frame record — every frame's interval, the time of each phase of the game's step and the events in it (FR-TOOL-230), written when a match ends, the app leaves the foreground or it is terminated (FR-TOOL-240) — and the run tool that takes it off the device and names the slowest frames (FR-TOOL-250). ### Q-340 — A bot's posture: standing, kneeling, lying

- **Found:** the maintainer on 2026-09-25, to be laid down for the future.
- **What diverged:** nothing yet; a bot is always standing. The maintainer wants a bot to choose its posture: standing — fast, the widest view, the least accurate fire; kneeling — middling speed, a middling view, fire better than average; lying — very slow, a low or narrow view, very accurate fire, and no grenades.
- **Still needed:** the numbers of each posture in the bot configuration (IF-BOT-010), and three things the specification does not have yet. And a view that is narrow needs a direction to be narrow around, which a bot does not have. Grenades a lying bot does not throw are FR-BOT-470's since 2026-10-11, and what a posture does to the throw is this entry's. Then how posture enters the choice of action (FR-BOT-080), and the figure drawn for each (FR-VIEW-070).

### Q-350 — Greenholm's sky shows grey-blue, not the blue it is configured with

- **Found:** the maintainer on the phone, 2026-09-25, through the free camera of debug mode; measured the same day on the simulator with the camera tilted to 10 degrees, its lowest (ADR-0031).
- **What diverged:** FR-GREENHOLM-020 asks for a daylight sky, and the sky shows as a pale grey-blue. The picture is applied — painted pure red, the sky came out red, 0.89, 0.13, 0 — and its colour space is honoured: repainted in linear light, it came out the same to the third decimal. What washes it is RealityKit's default tone mapping, which runs over the whole frame and pulls light, saturated colours towards grey. About 14 degrees above the horizon, near the top of that frame, the configured gradient is about 0.73, 0.83, 0.95 and the screen shows 0.70, 0.78, 0.84 — its blue and green held down, the colour pulled grey; at the horizon it is 0.80, 0.88, 0.96 and shows 0.75, 0.80, 0.85. `ToneMappingComponent` (iOS 27.0) on the camera changes it: an exposure of −1 darkens the sky and leaves it as grey, 0.53, 0.61, 0.68 at the top; a flat curve — toe, shoulder and angle all zero — gives the sky its blue, 0.77, 0.87, 0.96 at the top and 0.84, 0.90, 0.97 at the horizon, and washes out everything else: the shore goes white, the water a sharp cyan, the walls, grass and stairs pale. The match camera never shows the sky, so a match is not touched by any of it (ADR-0031).
- **Still needed:** the maintainer left it as it is on 2026-09-25 and chose among three when it is taken up: give the sky, in `config/setting/greenholm.json`, deeper and more saturated colours that land on the intended ones after the tone mapping, which changes the sky and nothing else and is settled by eye; tune the tone mapping for the whole game through `ToneMappingComponent`, which changes the look of everything and means settling the sun, the ambient and the kits' colours again; or keep the grey. The probe that measured it tilts the free camera to its lowest at the start of a match — `session.tiltCamera(by:screenHeight:)` with a large negative number — and was not kept.

### Q-390 — Should the map move to hexagonal cells?

### Q-570 — A full answer costs a fifth of the frame rate and answers three times late

- **Found:** on 2026-10-11, measuring the commander on the phone for the first time.
- **What the runs show.** Four 45-second runs on the iPhone 16 Pro, the same build each time but for the commander, the frame record marking where each call started and where its answer was taken. Thermal `nominal` throughout all four, so none of it is throttling.

  | run | frames | over 25 ms | frames a second | median interval | the app's own work, median / p95 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Release, no commander | 2599 | 46 (1.8 %) | 58.4 | 16.7 ms | 0.99 / 1.68 ms |
  | Release, commander | 2037 | 422 (20.7 %) | 45.7 | 16.9 ms | 1.73 / 3.06 ms |
  | Debug, no commander | 2577 | 62 (2.4 %) | 58.0 | 16.7 ms | 1.04 / 1.80 ms |
  | Debug, commander | 1986 | 495 (24.9 %) | 44.6 | 17.0 ms | 1.50 / 2.70 ms |

  Three things are worth reading off it. **The median frame is unchanged** — 16.7 against 16.9 ms — so the frame rate falls because a fifth of the frames are long, not because every frame got slower. **The commander's own work inside a frame is sub-millisecond**: building the state and taking an answer moves the median from 0.99 to 1.73 ms, which cannot make a 25-millisecond frame. The time goes somewhere the app is not running, which is the device doing the inference. And **Release changes almost nothing**, so none of it is the build.

  On latency, the same runs: a call answered in 19.2 and 13.1 seconds in Release, 12 to 27 in Debug, against a commander interval of 7 seconds and the 5 to 10 the requirement allows.
- **What these runs cannot say.** They are not measurements in this project's sense. `specs/50-verification.md` gives method `A` an unattended run along a fixed route, on this phone with a 20 % margin — a frame under 13.3 ms where the requirement says 16.6 — with Instruments' Game Performance Overview as the instrument. The game has no autopilot to drive such a route; the spike's instrument cannot run without the spike's app. **So no verdict on NFR-PERF-030, NFR-PERF-040 or NFR-PERF-010 is available from them**, including the control, which sits at 58 frames a second rather than 60 in an app nobody is steering. What they are is a price comparison between two builds measured the same way, which is what that document says a change is priced by.
- **What points at the answer's size.** The bench, on the Mac, with the same instructions and the same states and only the schema changed: 1.8 seconds median with its own five-field answer, 5.6 with the game's. The per-bot roles and weights IF-CMD-020 asks for are 397 output tokens against about 40, and output tokens are where the time goes.
- **One thing was tried and did not pay.** The two commanders were serialised, on the reading that FR-CMD-080's half-interval stagger stops meaning anything once an answer outlasts the interval and that the device was running two inferences at once for most of the run. It changed nothing: 495 frames over budget of 1986 against 481 of 1998. The change was reverted.
- **Half of it was settled on 2026-10-12 by ADR-0085**, and not as a performance trade: the maintainer's rule that a commander is a strategist took the per-bot roles and weights out of the answer, and what was left is four times cheaper to generate. On the phone, thermal `nominal`, the same arena: an answer now takes 3.5 to 4.7 seconds against 17.6 and 13.8, which is **inside** the interval for the first time and inside the 5 to 10 seconds NFR-PERF-040 allows. The late-answer half of this entry is therefore closed.
  **The frame rate half is not.** 43.8 frames a second against 43.7, unmoved, because a call is not started while the last is still out: the device generated for about 31 seconds of 45 before and about 40 of 45 now. The saving went into answering five times as often, and the only lever left is the interval — `commanderInterval`, 7 seconds, a tuning number in `config/match.json`.
- **Still needed:** the maintainer's word on the interval, and the autopilot. Four ways, and the first is the only one that keeps both the interval and the frame rate the specification asks for. Drop the per-bot roles and weights and go back to something near what the spike measured at 2.7 seconds. Ask for leans only for the bots the commander wants to move. Split into a cheap call on the interval and an expensive one rarely. Or keep the shape and change what the specification asks of the clock and of the frame.
  And, whichever is chosen, an autopilot — without one no number here can ever become a verdict.

### Q-590 — A session per call throws away the cache and reloads the model forty times a run

- **Found:** on 2026-10-11, profiling the game on the phone with Instruments' **Foundation Models** template — the instrument WWDC26 session 243 names for this, and the one the project had never used. It asks for consent before recording, because it captures the prompts and responses themselves, and waits on standard input until it gets it; a run started without an answer on that input hangs rather than records, and `--no-prompt` is what a recording from a script passes instead (`specs/50-verification.md`).
- **What it shows.** Ninety seconds of the shipping build, fifteen real inferences:

  | | median | range |
  |---|---|---|
  | inference | 9.8 s | 4.5 – 19.0 s |
  | prompt tokens | 768 | 756 – 778 |
  | response tokens | 227 | 221 – 500 |
  | **cached input tokens** | **0** | **0 – 0** |

  And beside it, the model loading table: **41 unloaded → loaded transitions and 42 loaded → unloaded in the same ninety seconds**, 7.6 seconds of loading in all. The model is thrashed in and out of memory about three times per inference.
- **Why it is this way.** FR-CMD-025 opens a session of its own for every call, so there is no transcript for a key-value cache to live in and nothing to keep the model resident between calls. WWDC26's guidance is the other way round: appending to a session preserves the cache and minimises time to first token, while rewriting history invalidates it. The same bench comparison on the Mac, with the game's own instructions and schema, bears the first half out — a kept session reads 929 of 1329 input tokens from cache where a fresh one reads none.
  What it does **not** bear out is that this buys speed here: kept, fresh and prewarmed came in at 6.4, 6.7 and 8.2 seconds mean over nine calls each, with single calls ranging 4.2 to 11.2 seconds. At that spread the three are not distinguishable.
- **Two things worth fixing whatever is decided about the session.** `prewarm(promptPrefix:)` exists in the installed SDK and is what the forty-one reloads are asking for. And two of the fifteen answers came back at exactly 500 response tokens — the ceiling Q-560 raised them to — so some answers are being truncated at the limit rather than finishing.
- **Three of Apple's levers were tried on 2026-10-12, and the phone paid for none of them.**

  | lever | what it bought |
  |---|---|
  | `prewarm`, warming the next session as soon as the last answered | 37 model loads against 41, with the loading time and the median call both a little worse — no difference at one run each |
  | the soldier's field names shortened to words of three or four letters | output 371 tokens against 395, about 6 % |
  | the same names shortened to single letters | output 369 — nothing beyond the short words |

  The field-name arithmetic had predicted about 30 % and was wrong because tokens are not characters: `selfSacrifice` costs a couple of tokens rather than thirteen characters' worth. The latency column is left out of the table on purpose — 5393, 5173 and 4330 milliseconds of median over twelve calls each, against single calls spanning 4.2 to 11.2 seconds, is not a reading.

  What this leaves is the shape. Apple's levers are all aimed at the time before the first token — the cache, the warm model, the short prompt — and the time here is spent generating 227 tokens, four fifths of which is field names and punctuation that constrained decoding has to emit whatever they are called.
- **Still needed:** a decision on FR-CMD-025, which was written on the spike's measurement that one session per call beat a kept one on tokens, latency and robustness. That measurement stands on its own numbers; what is new is that it was taken with a five-field answer and before the framework reported caching at all, and that the cost it does not count is forty model loads a run. Re-measure on the phone with the Foundation Models instrument, prewarmed and kept, before changing the requirement — and raise or remove the response ceiling either way.

- **What was measured.** Over a hundred and fifty seconds of the shipped arena with nobody steering, `fire sidearm` was chosen zero times and `close` zero times. Every bot kept both its spare pistol magazines to the end, and five of the eight died with a full pistol on them. Measured again after ADR-0093: the pistol is fired and `close` is still zero.
- **Why.** With the primary dry, firing the pistol at four metres is worth about 0.16 — the attack weight 2.0, by a nearness of 0.33 within the assault's six-metre detection range, by the pistol's strength share of 0.5, by aggression and courage — against a reload at 0.78 while a spare is left, and a flight at about 0.25 once none is. And because the pistol's six metres cover everything a bot can see at six, nothing a bot sees is ever out of reach of something, which is the condition FR-BOT-570 is built on: the rush is never offered.
- **Why it matters.** Two requirements are built and exercised only by their tests. FR-BOT-460 gives each weapon an action so that the score and not a rule decides which is used, and the score has decided that one of the four is never used at all.
- **Still needed:** for the rush, the maintainer's judgement on the phone, and then one number: the detection range against the pistol's range — while a bot sees no further than its shortest weapon reaches, nothing it sees is ever out of reach and the rush cannot be offered. It is tuning (IF-BOT-010) and not a change to a requirement.

### Q-690 — A grenade seen more often costs the snake its life

- **Found:** on 2026-10-13, building the weapon's own clock (ADR-0093), from the maintainer's "I still do not see any grenades".
- **What was measured.** Raising `utilityTermWeights.blast` from 1.2 to 1.8 — the weight at which a grenade thrown at two bodies is worth more than a shot from a full primary — more than doubled the grenades thrown and killed the snake in under eight seconds of the `room` map, with one bot a side as surely as with four. The snake never ate anybody in any of the four scenarios measured; at 1.2 it ate at frame 378 of 1800. Two tests of the frame record and the restart were red for that reason and no other.
- **Why.** The score of a throw counts bodies, and a snake is one body in it; the damage counts parts, and a blast raises what it deals by every part of the snake it covers (FR-BOT-490). So the weight that is right against bots is several times too much against the player.
- **What was decided for now.** The weight stays at 1.2, and the grenades the maintainer asked to see came from somewhere else: the slots that stopped being wasted took grenade frames from 105 to 250 with the weight untouched (ADR-0093).
- **Still needed:** the maintainer's eye on the phone on whether 250 frames of grenade in a hundred and fifty seconds is enough to see. If it is not, the number to move is not this weight but the blast's own damage against the snake (`furtherPartShare`, or the grenade's damage), since the same weight cannot be right for a body and for a six-segment player.
