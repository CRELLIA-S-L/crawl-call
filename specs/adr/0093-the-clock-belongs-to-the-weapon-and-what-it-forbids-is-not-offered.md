# ADR-0093 — The clock belongs to the weapon, and what it forbids is not offered

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-13
- **Related requirements:** FR-BOT-600, FR-BOT-610, FR-BOT-440, FR-BOT-450, FR-BOT-460, FR-BOT-170, FR-BOT-510, FR-BOT-520, FR-BOT-540, FR-BOT-500, FR-BOT-570, IF-BOT-010, NFR-PERF-020

## Context and problem statement

The maintainer read one line of `Execution` — `now - bots[index].lastShot >= knife.numbers.timeBetweenStrikes` — and said what they expected instead: a bot an object with weapon slots, a weapon an object with a common interface carrying its own times of action, its ways of being used, its stock of uses and the numbers of each use.

That line reaches three owners in one expression: the match's second, a field on the bot, a number in the catalogue. Nobody owned the question it answers. The numbers of a weapon had moved to the weapon on 2026-10-11 (FR-BOT-450, ADR-0086), and the clock those numbers are read against had stayed on the bot — one field for everything a bot carries. What that cost was measured over a hundred and fifty seconds of the shipped arena: a bot chose to strike, the strike was refused because a second had not passed, the bot stood, and at the next slot it chose to strike again. Four strikes in five did nothing, the pistol was never fired in a whole match, and the first deaths came at fifteen seconds.

## Decisions

**A use of a weapon is one named value.** `Arms.Use` — a shot from a named slot, a grenade thrown, a strike, a knife thrown. One enumeration and not a protocol the weapons conform to, for three reasons that are checked rather than guessed: the set is closed, since a kind of weapon is a table in the catalogue and a type beside it (IF-BOT-010), so there is no openness to buy; a bot is a value compared whole, and an existential would take `Equatable` from `Arms` and from `Bot` with it, which is what a repeatable match stands on; and an allocation for every weapon of every bot in every frame runs the wrong way against a deliberation budget of half a millisecond already measured at twice that on the phone (NFR-PERF-020).

**The clock is the weapon's, not the use's and not the bot's.** `Arms.Piece` — a firearm in a slot, the pouch, the knife — keys `lastUsed`, and `Arms.between(_:)` reads that piece's time out of the catalogue. So the knife struck with and the knife thrown wait on one time, which FR-BOT-610's statement obliges in the words "that weapon" and not "that use". It was built keyed by the use first, and the measurement said why the statement is right: a bot one metre from its enemy struck, and at the next slot threw away the only knife it had, because the throw carries no time of its own and its score does not fall with a magazine. One clock a weapon, and a bot that has just struck keeps the knife it is about to strike with again.

**What is left on the bot is the slot's guard and nothing else.** `Bot.lastShot` now answers one question — whether this bot has already acted inside a deliberation period — which is a property of deliberation and not of a gun (FR-BOT-170, ADR-0057). Two clocks in one field was the fault; two clocks in two fields is the fix.

**One predicate, asked by both halves of a frame.** `Arms.ready(_:at:)` answers "may this use be taken now": something left of it, and the weapon's own time passed. The deliberation asks it before scoring and the execution asks it before acting, so an action that cannot be taken is not scored rather than scored, chosen and then refused (FR-BOT-600). This is the third case of a rule the project already followed twice — a firearm with no round is not an action (FR-BOT-460), a reload with no spare magazine is not one (FR-BOT-540), a throw onto a squadmate is not one (FR-BOT-500) — and the only one that needs the match's second in the score, which is why `Deliberation.Situation` gained `now`.

**Every use is asked, including the ones that look exempt.** The knife's throw was left out of the score at first, on the reasoning that a throw has no time to wait out. The measurement found a bot choosing the throw and standing for twenty-five slots running: the execution asked the predicate, the score did not, and the gap between them is exactly the fault FR-BOT-600 names.

**The rush is not a use.** `FR-BOT-570`'s cells stay as they were: crossing the ground to where a weapon would reach is not using it, and a knife still on its time reaches by the time the bot arrives.

## Numbers the maintainer set

**The knife strikes once every five seconds** (`timeBetweenStrikes`, 1.0 → 5.0): twenty damage a second at the reach of an arm was double a rifle's and what made a bot stand in melee instead of fighting. At five seconds it is four damage a second — the weakest thing a bot carries by rate, and the only one that never runs out (FR-BOT-510, FR-BOT-520). This is the number ADR-0088 shipped at one second, and it is a number and not that decision: the knife still has a time of its own, read from the catalogue, and only its value moved.

**The grenade's weight stays at 1.2.** Raising `utilityTermWeights.blast` to 1.8 was proposed from the arithmetic — a grenade on two bodies would then be worth more than a shot from a full primary — and the arithmetic left out what a blast does to the snake: twenty-five damage raised by every part of it covered (FR-BOT-490). Measured in `room`, at 1.8 the squads killed the snake in under eight seconds, in two bots as surely as in eight, and the snake never ate at all. The weight stays where it was, and the grenades the maintainer asked to see come from the slots that stopped being wasted: over the same hundred and fifty seconds, grenade frames went from 105 to 250 with the weight untouched.

## Consequences

The shipped arena, a hundred and fifty seconds, nobody steering, against the same measurement before this change: the first deaths move from 15 s to 33 s and four bots live to the end instead of three; `fire sidearm` goes from nothing to 381 frames and `reload sidearm` from nothing to 182, so the pistol is a weapon in this game for the first time; grenades go from 105 to 250; strikes go from 708 to 142, and each of those is a strike that lands rather than a bot standing. `close` is still nothing, which is Q-680 and not this.

Three tests had their premises re-posed, each for a named reason, none weakened. `KnifeTests` now reads the slot after a strike as a shot rather than a second strike. The two tests that need the snake to eat somebody — the meal in the frame record and the restart that puts an eaten bot back on screen — lost the premise that the snake survives a firefight long enough to eat: they were passing by a hair before this change (the snake ate at frame 398 of 1800 with two segments left) and now pose their meal with one bot a side, through one helper in `TestSupport` where there had been two decoders of the same configuration. `CommandedMatchTests` compares an order east with an order west instead of an order with silence: a squad under no order advances on the enemy it believes in (FR-BOT-360) and the enemy of red is east, so with bots living longer the silent squad drifts east by itself — measured at 14.25 m against 14.24 m for the commanded one, no margin at all, while east against west is 14.24 m against 5.04 m in an arena whose east is 13.38 m.

It costs the deliberation twice what it did, and not for the predicate: measured on the phone over forty-five seconds either side of this change, 0.461 ms to 0.892 ms in the frames deliberation runs in, level for the first fifteen seconds and apart from the moment the old world's bots start dying. A living bot is scored against every enemy it sees and every cell its squadmates claim, so a match that does not thin out is a dearer match to think in. Nothing was late more often for it, the numbers are in Q-460, and NFR-PERF-020 — which is the budget that would defer slots rather than exceed the time — is still deferred.

What is not done and was considered: the four weapon actions of `Action` — `fire`, `throwGrenade`, `strike`, `throwKnife` — are not collapsed into one `use`. It would turn four hand-written blocks in the deliberation and four in the execution into one each, and it changes no behaviour, so it is an ADR and not a requirement; it would also re-point twenty-one mentions in three source files and forty-four in fifteen test files, which is a pass of its own and not one to take while numbers are being tuned.
